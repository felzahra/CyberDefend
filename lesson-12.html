Section 01

VoIP Overview

Voice over IP transforms analog voice into digital packets. Proper VLAN segmentation and QoS are critical for toll-quality voice.

Voice VLAN

Separates voice (VLAN 100) from data (VLAN 200) on the same switch port using 802.1Q tagging.

QoS Marking

DSCP EF (46) for voice, AF41 for signaling. Priority queuing ensures low latency under congestion.

PoE Power

802.3af (15.4W) or 802.3at (25.5W) powers IP phones directly from the switch.

Section 05

DSL Integration with VoIP

Connecting VoIP over DSL requires bandwidth planning, proper MTU, and upstream QoS shaping.

DSL Types for VoIP

VDSL2Up to 100Mbps
ADSL2+Up to 24Mbps
SDSLUp to 2.3Mbps sym

Bandwidth per Call

G.711 (uncompressed): ~87 kbps

G.729 (compressed): ~24 kbps

G.722 (HD voice): ~87 kbps

Rule: Reserve 30% overhead for signaling, STUN, and burst.

DSL Considerations

  • •MTU reduced to 1492 (PPPoE) — fragmentation kills voice
  • •Interleaving adds latency — request fast-path
  • •Upstream bandwidth is the bottleneck
  • •Jitter buffer must compensate for DSL variance

Common Issues & Fixes

01

Phone stuck on "Configuring VLAN"

Check CDP/LLDP-MED is enabled. Verify DHCP Option 176/242 has correct VLAN ID.

02

Choppy audio on DSL

Enable shaping to 80% of upstream. Use G.729. Request fast-path from ISP.

03

PC behind phone loses connectivity

Verify native VLAN matches data VLAN (200). Check port-security max is at least 3.

04

PoE phone powers up then resets

Check total PoE budget. Color phones may need 802.3at. Verify cable length.
